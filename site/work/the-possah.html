Case 02 · Fashion · Bangalore

The budget didn't cover a studio.

Haute couture
Sector
2025
Year
Identity · campaign · site
Scope

So we built one. Then we built the system it had to live inside.

The problem

A women's boutique with a full collection to launch and no budget for a studio shoot. They needed a campaign that looked like money had been spent, and a website that didn't lose the sale.

The collection needed a campaign. The budget didn't cover a studio. So the campaign was built rather than booked — models generated, directed and graded to one light, across twenty-four pieces.

That solved the pictures. It did not solve the harder thing: a couture label where every piece is one of one still has to sound like one label, on a swing tag, in a caption, and in a line of copy written at midnight by someone who was not in the room when the brand was decided.

01 · Built, not booked

Twenty-four pieces, no studio.

A studio day buys you a location, a crew, a model and a backdrop that does not move. The budget bought none of it, so the campaign was generated instead — models, rooms and daylight built to a direction, across twenty-four pieces.

The models are generated, and the rooms with them · the garments are real · scope on file: AI model photoshoot, 24 SKUs, as recorded on NUANSE's current site

02 · The fix

The voice, held.

Possah's guidelines put the brand at one point on a two-axis matrix: bold, and composed. Drag the point away from it. The six lines the label writes dissolve into the six it refuses to write. A brand's tone is a position, and this is what the other positions cost.

03 · The system

Eleven sections.

Brand story, logo system, colour, typography, moodboard, content direction, social, packaging, web, stationery, tone of voice. A guidelines document is only worth the arguments it ends, so every number in it is specified rather than described.

  • Warm Ivory#F4ECDF · 244 236 223 Primary. Sixty per cent of any composition — the ground everything is allowed to be quiet on.
  • Deep Forest Green#1F3A2D · 31 58 45 Primary. Thirty per cent. The colour that does the holding.
  • Dusty Rose Pink#C99A99 · 201 154 153 Primary. Softness held with strength — and the only one of the five that clears contrast as text on the green.
  • Burnt Orange#B35A2B · 179 90 43 Accent. Part of the ten per cent, with the gold.
  • Muted Mustard Gold#C8973A · 200 151 58 Accent. The other part of the ten.

The accent discipline is testable, so we tested it. Sampling all thirty-three guideline boards against the five documented palette colours, accent runs a median of 1.7 per cent of palette-carrying pixels against the brand's own 10 per cent ceiling, and stays under it on 31 of 33. The two over are the colour page itself, where the accent swatches are the subject, and the moodboard, which is photography rather than layout. Method and raw figures: tools/sample-ratios.py.

04 · Naming

A grammar, not a list.

Couture produces a new collection faster than it produces a new brand. So the names were given a rule rather than a list, and the label was split into four lines that each know what they are for.

05 · It travels

Where it is touched.

A couture piece is bought in a room and opened at home. Both are the brand, and the second one is the one people photograph.

06 · On a screen

Not losing the sale.

The site was audited before it was redesigned: user experience and the technical side, then the made-to-measure page, which is where a couture label either explains itself or loses the order. The campaign the budget could not buy is what fills it.
